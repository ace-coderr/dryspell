# Dryspell

Parametric weather cover on Arbitrum. A buyer picks a location, a peril (drought or flood) and a payout. Dryspell prices the cover on-chain, locks the premium into an underwriter pool, and pays out automatically when real weather data crosses the trigger. No claims form, no adjuster.

Built for the Arbitrum Open House Dubai Online Buildathon.

**Status: working testnet prototype on Arbitrum Sepolia.** Contracts are deployed and verified, the site is live at https://ace-coderr.github.io/dryspell/, and everything on it reads the deployed contracts. All money is TestUSD play money. The pricing engine currently running is the **Solidity fallback**, not Stylus (see below). The full write-up of the product, judging criteria and setup is still being finished.

## Engine mode: read this first

The pricing and solvency engine is written twice, with one ABI:

| Engine | Where | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stylus (Rust)** | [contracts/stylus](contracts/stylus) | Complete: 38 tests, builds to WASM. **Not deployed:** Stylus activation was paused on Arbitrum Sepolia, Arbitrum One and the Robinhood Chain testnet when this was built (2026-11-21). |
| **Solidity fallback** | [contracts/solidity/src/DryspellEngineSol.sol](contracts/solidity/src/DryspellEngineSol.sol) | Live fallback with identical selectors, errors and arithmetic, proven equal on 6,648 vectors generated by the Rust code. Reports itself as `solidity-fallback`. |

The app shows which engine is live, read from the chain. Stylus is never claimed unless the deployed engine answers `"stylus"`. See [contracts/solidity/README.md](contracts/solidity/README.md).

## What the demo shows, and what it does not

- **Replay is not a live payout.** The settled policy on the ledger (Garissa short rains 2023, flood) is a **Replay**: a finished historical season replayed on the test network by the owner so a payout can be shown end to end. Its trigger was chosen knowing the outcome, and replays are hard-wired to test networks by chain id. Every Replay is tagged "Replay" in the app and flagged `replay = true` in every event. The live forward-season policy (Jodhpur monsoon 2027) cannot settle before that season ends.
- **Stylus is not live.** Stylus activation was paused on Arbitrum Sepolia when this was deployed, so the Solidity fallback prices and caps the book. The app and the MCP server read the engine mode from the chain and say `solidity-fallback`.
- **The oracle is v1 and trusted at one point.** Bonded reporters post both sources' readings and a dispute window follows, but a single named resolver rules on disputes and on contested policies (where the two sources fall on opposite sides of a trigger or too far apart). The resolver can choose a wrong result. Decentralised resolution is the next step.
- **Admin powers on the testnet deployment.** One owner key controls the contracts, and the **same address is also the named resolver** (a separate resolver would need a third key to manage). It cannot move vault funds, change a policy, re-point the engine's book or vault (set-once in v4), or alter calibration or season windows once set, but it can pause new sales, load pricing tables and parameters, and change the resolver. A production deployment would put the owner behind a multisig and timelock and give the resolver its own key. Testnet timings (1-hour dispute window and vault hold) are much shorter than production would use.
- **Basis risk.** Both weather sources are gridded products tens of kilometres wide: cover pays on the data, not on any one farm. The Backtest page shows where it would have missed.
- **Remaining look-ahead.** Calibration factors use 1991-2019 only, so no season that can be settled (2020 onward) feeds its own calibration. The pricing tables (the fits behind the premium) and the source-agreement gate are fitted on all 35 seasons, so a Replay is priced with a fit that includes the replayed season. Forward seasons do not have this overlap.
- **Thin history.** About 35 seasons per place: tail odds carry real sampling error, which the price loads for (the uncertainty margin).

## Data sources

Dryspell is priced from real daily rainfall, 1991-2025, for 12 locations across West Africa, East Africa, South Asia and Southeast Asia, from two independent sources: Open-Meteo (ECMWF ERA5, CC BY 4.0, free non-commercial tier) and NASA POWER (cite service and access date). Terms, attribution, method and limits are in [data/README.md](data/README.md).
